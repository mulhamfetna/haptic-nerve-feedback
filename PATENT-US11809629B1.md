- **Assignee:** Afference Inc., Lafayette CO · **Sole inventor:** Jacob Segil (Boulder CO)
- **Filed** 2022-06-10 (App. 17/838,116) · **Granted** 2023-11-07 · Term extension: 0 days

## 1. Core concept and vocabulary (worth adopting in our own docs)

The patent names the device class **RSTS — "Referred Sensation Transcutaneous Stimulation"
device**, and the effect a **"transient synesthetic event"**: electrically stimulating a
sensory nerve at one body location so the brain perceives a sensation at a *different,
distal* location that the nerve innervates.

Mechanism as described: transdermal electrical signals locally augment the membrane
potential of the nerve → depolarization that **mimics afferent signaling from the distal
area** → the brain cannot distinguish it from a real touch event at that distal site.

Three terms are kept rigorously separate, and we should too:

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Stimulation site** | where the electrodes inject current (e.g., proximal phalanx) |
| **Sense impression site** | where the sensation is *perceived* (e.g., fingertip pad) |
| **Sense impression modality** | the type of feeling: pressure, paresthesia/tingling, vibration, temperature change, texture, time-varying mechanical |

Canonical example: ring on the proximal phalanx of the index finger stimulates a branch /
cross-section of the **median nerve** passing through the phalanx → pressure perceived at
the index fingertip pad. (FIG 9A marks the target zone on the *side* of the proximal
phalanx — where the palmar digital nerve runs — just distal to the finger crease.)

## 2. The software architecture (this is the actual invention)

The independent claim is not "electrically stimulate a nerve from a ring" — that alone is
prior art. The claimed invention is the **three-profile architecture**:

1. **Stimulation profile** — data store mapping (modality, site) → signal parameters:
   current magnitude, polarity, pulse width, frequency, duty cycle. Pressure-at-fingertip
   and temperature-at-fingertip are *different profiles*; same modality at a different
   site is also a different profile.
2. **Calibration profile** — where the electrodes actually sit relative to the nerve *for
   this wearing session*. Ring rotation changes everything, so this is rebuilt or updated
   regularly (per wear, even hourly/daily — skin impedance drifts through the day).
3. **User-specific profile** — per-user maximum current / pain-threshold ceiling; every
   stimulation plan is clipped below it regardless of modality, site, or calibration.

**Stimulation plan** = stimulation profile + calibration profile, clipped by the user
profile → concrete signal(s) applied across a *selected pair* (or sets) of electrodes,
with anode/cathode roles, magnitudes, and ordering assigned per electrode.

### Calibration workflow (FIGS 5, 8, 13, 14 — directly reusable as our protocol)

- **Orientation calibration:** iterate a prefixed pattern of anode/cathode pairings with
  small, safe test signals, pausing between each; the wearer presses a "**PRESS ON
  SENSATION**" button (phone app) the moment a referred sensation is felt. The pairing
  that fired reveals electrode-to-nerve geometry.
- **Stimulation-profile calibration:** using that orientation, iterate pulse width /
  frequency / amplitude; the app collects structured feedback — buttons for "more/less
  localized than previous", modality selection (thermal / pressure / texture), and
  "painful / approaching painful" (which sets the user threshold). Voice "yes/no" is an
  alternative input.
- Optionally, a **trained ML model** (labels: "sensation received / not received" vs
  calibration parameters, across users, conditioned on demographics, temperature,
  humidity, geography) proposes electrode selections and parameters instead of brute
  iteration.
- Sensors feeding recalibration: **skin impedance** (updates the stimulation envelope in
  real time), temperature, humidity, conductivity, IMU.

**Replication note:** this is exactly the "sensation library + logging protocol" we
planned as software stage S2 — the patent validates the architecture and gives us the UX
pattern for the calibration app.

## 3. Hardware details worth copying (or consciously deviating from)

- **Electrode array on the ring's inner surface**, either a small arc segment (FIG 4A) or
  fully circumferential (FIG 4B). Layout variants: single row, interleaved
  anode/cathode of different materials (FIG 4D), two aligned rows (4E), two *offset* rows
  (4F), circular electrodes (4G). Any electrode can be software-assigned anode, cathode,
  or left floating; multiple anodes per cathode and vice versa ("current steering vector").
- **Materials:** biocompatible metal, gold preferred; avoid nickel and silver (allergy
  risk); oxidation-resistant alloys listed: copper alloys, gold alloys (AgNW, AgCl),
  tungsten alloys (CuNW), titanium alloys. Rigid or flexible/polymer electrodes; may sit
  slightly proud of the surface to guarantee skin contact.
- **Housing:** elastic inner surface worn in tension (keeps contact), rigid outer cap;
  adjustable diameter; multi-finger variants contemplated.
- **Signal generation:** can be largely software — DAC or shift register iterating
  waveform sample points, or a direct digital synthesizer; square or triangular waves
  with digitally set frequency, duty cycle, amplitude, phase.
- **Comms:** Bluetooth or **Ultra-Wideband** to the phone (also Wi-Fi, 5G, 433 MHz, IR,
  acoustic contemplated). Power: battery (plus exotic options: Peltier, piezo, solar,
  inductive).

## 4. System integration (maps 1:1 onto our software roadmap)

- Phone/PC "client device" sits between a **virtual environment** (environment engine +
  haptic data stream source) and the RSTS device. The spec gives an example **haptic
  event as JSON**: per-fingertip grams of pressure, and per-phalanx entries of
  `{modality, value, duration}` — e.g. pressure `0.5g` for `0.2s` plus temperature
  `−3deg`, to simulate gripping a metal trigger. A ready-made schema idea for our S2/S3.
- **Timing trick (FIG 16):** deliberately fire the tactile impression ~100 ms (up to
  hundreds of ms) *before* the corresponding audio/visual cue — real somatosensory
  contact is felt before it is heard, and this desync increases realism.
- **Teleoperation/prosthetics loop (FIG 17):** robot or prosthetic senses
  texture/pressure/temperature → referred stimulation plan executes. (Our USECASES.md
  robot-teleoperation line, patented as an application.)
- **Notifications (FIG 18):** phone events rendered as sensation patterns (two taps on
  the back of the left hand for an email; a circle traced on the palm for a specific
  sender). Recorded/replayed social touch (a grandparent records a heart traced on a
  palm; a child replays it later).
- Other described uses: surgeon feedback in robot-assisted surgery, navigation by
  directional taps or thermal cues (cold = wrong way, warm = right way), finger guidance
  for instruments, virtual keyboards on flat surfaces (FIG 1 shows exactly this),
  authentication by which-finger-was-stimulated, counter-signaling to suppress tremors,
  combined motor + sensory stimulation (finger flexion + fingertip pressure = feeling of
  a real object deflecting the finger), *moving* impressions (pressure sliding from
  fingertip toward palm), multiplexed modalities (pressure + cold simultaneously), and
  **vestibular stimulation** from a behind-the-ear wearable to induce imbalance /
  vertigo for horror or suspense media (FIGS 7D, 15).
- Implantable variants are explicitly contemplated (finger and wrist implants, FIG 9),
  cooperating with the wearable — their bridge toward our H3/H4 territory.

## 5. Claims analysis — what is actually protected

**Only one independent claim (claim 1)**, a *device* claim. To infringe, a device must
have **all** of: skin-contoured housing; electrode array through its surface; memory +
processor running software that (a) determines a sense impression site **distal** to the
stimulation site, (b) determines the modality, (c) queries a **first data store** for the
stimulation profile (magnitude + polarity), (d) queries a **second data store** for the
calibration profile (electrode locations as currently worn), (e) queries a **third data
store** for a user-specific current-magnitude threshold, (f) builds a stimulation plan
across a **selected pair** of electrodes, and (g) executes it.

Dependent claims narrow to: annular/finger-worn (2), index/middle finger + median nerve
(3), fingertip impression site (4), wrist-worn (5), **two electrodes of different
metals** (6), max-current in user profile (7), commanded by a separate device (8) that is
a virtual environment (9), modality ∈ {pressure, temperature, texture, time-varying
mechanical} (10).

### What this means for us

- **Our H1 bench rig does not practice claim 1.** A fixed electrode pair driven by a
  manually tuned TENS unit has no electrode-pair *selection*, no queryable profile data
  stores, no calibration-profile architecture. Freedom to experiment is wide.
- The claim is **narrow**: all three profile data stores + pair selection are required
  elements. Simple published research systems (including the wrist tTENS literature in
  RESEARCH-LANDSCAPE.md §4) predate and surround it.
- **Where we'd converge with the claim** is precisely our S2 plan: a sensation library
  (= stimulation profiles) + per-wear calibration + per-user safety ceiling, on a ring
  with a selectable electrode array. Fine for open non-commercial research; if this ever
  becomes a *product*, a real FTO analysis is mandatory — and note Afference almost
  certainly has **continuation applications pending** (B1 grant from 2022 filing; the
  cited-art landscape — many Jung/Meta patents, Selker, Thota — shows a crowded field).
- The examiner-cited art (Jung US11402904 G06F 3/016, Selker US8917167, Chow, Daniels,
  Rosenbluth A61N 1/08…) is a ready-made reading list of adjacent approaches.

## 6. Concrete takeaways for the H1 prototype

1. **Electrode placement:** side of the proximal phalanx, just distal to the base crease
   (FIG 9A) — over the palmar digital nerve — not the finger pad.
2. **Start with a 2-electrode pair** around the phalanx; graduate to a multi-electrode
   ring (≥8 contacts, FIG 4B style) only when we need software-selectable orientation.
3. **Gold-plated or titanium contacts; no nickel.** Slightly proud of the housing, worn
   in slight tension.
4. **Calibration UX to copy:** iterate pairings at low fixed test amplitude → "press on
   sensation" → then parameter sweeps with localized/modality/pain-proximity feedback.
   Log everything (this doubles as our S2 dataset).
5. **Impedance sensing** is the key enabler for session-to-session repeatability — plan
   for a measurement channel early.
6. **Timing:** when we reach screen/VR stages, lead visuals with the tactile pulse by
   ~100 ms.
